# Email That Keeps Up With Your Family

# What if you got some help to manage your inbox?

- Soccer registration closes Friday.
- The dentist moved Tuesday's appointment.
- There's a permission slip somewhere in an email from the school.
- Your husband forwarded you the flight confirmation.
- Someone needs to order the teacher gift.
- The birthday party changed locations.
- Apparently you're supposed to remember to bring **two dozen individually wrapped, nut-free snacks** next Thursday.

It's all floating in your email.
How's that working for you?

Ever have days where there are more hours than you have discipline?

## Email remembers messages. You have to remember everything else.

Your inbox is very good at telling you what people **said**.

But its _not the best_ at telling you what you need to **do**.

You read an email from school, and make a mental note. You leave the pediatrician's message unread because that's how you
remember to call them. You star the soccer email. You text your husband a screenshot. You put something on the calendar.You leave another email in your inbox because you're waiting for someone to answer it.

Let's face it your inbox is trying to be a second brain - but its really just fancy post-it-notes.

It remembers every message you've ever received ---
but somehow **you** are expected to remember what matters across all that.

# What if email could keep up?

Imagine opening your email and instead of seeing:

**47 unread messages**

you saw:

**Three things need you today.**

> Soccer registration closes Friday. Want me to register Emma with the same information as last season?

> The dentist offered three new appointment times. Recommending "Tuesday at 3:30" since it doesn't conflict with travel times on other appointments in your calendar.

> You never got a response about the birthday-party deposit. Want to follow up with the email below?

That's different. Did your shoulders relax?

That's not an easier to organize inbox...

**That's an inbox that's helping.**

## It reads the email. You keep the responsibility.

Ask:

> What do I need to know from school this week?

> Did I ever send the camp medical form?

> When is that birthday party?

> Are we waiting on anyone before we book the trip?

> Find the email with the soccer uniform sizes from last year.

And get an answer.

Not ten search results for you to dig through.

An answer.

## Tell it what needs to happen.

You shouldn't have to write:

> Dear Mrs. Thompson,\
> Thank you so much for reaching out...

when what you actually mean is:

> Tell her Grace would love to come, but we need to leave by 4:30.

Tell your email what you want.

It can handle the words.

And when you're comfortable letting it do more:

> Find a time when all three kids can get dental cleanings.

> Get the details for summer camp and tell me what I still need to do.

> If Coach doesn't respond by Thursday, follow up.

> Handle the back-and-forth to reschedule this appointment.

You decide what it can do.

You decide what still needs your approval.

# You know you've arrived when...

You don't check email because you're afraid you've forgotten something.

You don't keep messages unread as reminders.

You don't search your inbox for "soccer" and open nine messages trying
to reconstruct what happened.

You don't have to remember who you're waiting on.

You don't wake up thinking:

**There was something in an email I was supposed to do...**

Your email knows what's happening.

It knows what's finished.

It knows what's waiting.

And most importantly:

**It knows when it actually needs you.**

Your family doesn't need another inbox manager.

They need you.

**Let your email manage the inbox.**
